## 1. For each game: what is the very first thing the player does, how soon, is there an intro cutscene, is there a tutorial, forced or skippable?

### Takeaway
The biggest 2025 hits (Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot) have no cutscene and no stepwise tutorial in any source read: the player spawns on their own plot or base with a tiny amount of money that buys exactly the cheapest item, and the first action is a purchase. Older or deeper games (Bee Swarm Simulator, Adopt Me, Fisch, Dig) use a short NPC or quest-led intro tied to one free starter item rather than a screen-blocking tutorial.

### Cited Findings

Grow a Garden
- Players begin with an empty plot of farmland among other players' plots and a currency called Sheckles, which they use to buy carrot seeds. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- The new player starts with 20 Sheckles; no seed is given free; the guide says "Since you only have 20 Sheckles, you should buy two Carrot Seeds." (implies a carrot seed costs 10). — [Slyther Games, May 2025](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- The only guidance described is that the game "points you towards the Seed Shop" when you first start; no cutscene or step list is described. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- Planting: equip the seed from the hotbar at the bottom of the screen, then click where to plant, inside one of eight plots (beds) in your garden. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- Buttons at the top centre of the screen teleport the player to the seller; gear and pet traders have no teleport button and sit on the opposite side of the area. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- A second write-up describes the start as "an empty plot and a single seed" (conflicts slightly with "20 Sheckles, buy two seeds"; may reflect a later version or loose wording). — [Kids Media Is Cool, Substack](https://kidsmediaiscool.substack.com/p/the-roblox-game-thats-hooked-22-million); contradicted in detail by [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- A 2026 guide lists the first session as four steps: claim your plot (given on joining), buy the cheapest seeds, plant everything, harvest/sell/rebuy. No tutorial is mentioned. — [gag.gg beginner guide, updated June 2026](https://gag.gg/guides/beginners-guide)

Steal a Brainrot
- Players begin with a base for storing Brainrots and a small amount of currency; a conveyor belt across the map sells Brainrots that generate income passively. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- The player spawns at "an empty starter base with very little money"; "a large red conveyor belt runs through the center of the map"; the only early steps described are check your base, go to the conveyor, buy the cheapest characters, bring them back and place them. No tutorial is mentioned. — [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/guides/166496-steal-a-brainrot-beginners-guide-tips-and-basics.html)
- The cheapest Brainrot, Noobini Pizzanini, costs $25 and earns $1 per second. — [allthings.how value list](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-values-belt-price-and-income-per-second-for-all-99-brainrots/)
- A guide summarises the opening as "Spawn, claim a base, and buy the first three Common brainrots." (site states it is AI-written from the Fandom wiki). — [rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/steal-a-brainrot/beginner-guide)

Pet Simulator 99
- A guide's six-step quick start: equip best pets and farm the newest area; "follow the quest arrows and rank goals"; claim free rewards (gifts, rank rewards, codes); buy cheap diamond upgrades; push to Area 25 and rebirth; visit the Trading Plaza. So PS99 does use arrows, but tied to a goal list rather than a blocking tutorial. — [U7BUY guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/)
- Claim (unsourced opinion article): "Players hatch their first egg in under 30 seconds", with rarity reveal and sound tuned to feel good even on common pulls. — [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/what-every-top-100-roblox-game-gets-right-a-design-analysis)

Adopt Me!
- Sir Woofington in the Nursery gives new players a free Starter Egg; the player must be in the adult (Parent) role to claim it. — [Adopt Me Wiki, Starter Egg](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Starter_Egg)
- The egg's first task is always "Hungry"; a free sandwich is given each time the player joins and the game explains how to feed it to the egg. — [Adopt Me Wiki](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Starter_Egg)
- The Starter Egg can only hatch a Dog or a Cat (guaranteed common), cannot be traded and cannot be obtained again. — [Adopt Me Wiki](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Starter_Egg)
- The game opens with a role choice (baby or parent/pet) made by clicking a button (older, low-quality guide). — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/how-get-started-adopt-me-roblox-a-step-by-step-guide)

Bee Swarm Simulator
- Black Bear is one of the first quest givers, standing between the Red Cannon and the Special Sprout Summoner, close to the Sunflower Field and the hives. His first quest "Sunflower Start" asks for 100 pollen from the Sunflower Field (reward 200 honey); second "Dandelion Deed" asks for 250 pollen from the Dandelion Field (400 honey); third "Pollen Fetcher" 500 pollen anywhere (500 honey). — [Bee Swarm Simulator Wiki, Black Bear](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Bear)
- Mother Bear stands under a striped awning at the far end of the Sunflower Field; her first quest "Treat Tutorial" gives 1 Treat on accept, asks the player to raise a bee to level 2 and feed 1 treat, and pays 250 honey, 3 treats, 1 ticket. Her opening line is "Hello there new beekeeper!" — [Bee Swarm Simulator Wiki, Mother Bear](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Mother_Bear)
- The first step is hatching a Basic Egg (Basic Bee or a random bee; odds Rare 10%, Epic 2.5%, Legendary 0.5%, Gifted 0.4%). Quest log opens from a "?" icon top-left; pollen and honey bars are top-middle. — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/roblox-bee-swarm-simulator-how-play-bees-bears)

Fisch
- Moosewood is the starting island; the game has a tutorial which the reviewer calls "pretty good" but incomplete. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fisch-roblox-beginners-guide/)
- Fishing is a three-part action: cast (release timing sets distance), shake (click the circles; each click cuts the bite wait by about 0.5 s; optional), then a reel minigame keeping the fish marker inside a control bar until a progress bar fills. A Perfect Catch pays C$10 and 1.5 XP bonus. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fisch-roblox-beginners-guide/)

Dig (2025)
- The tutorial "does a decent job of covering the basics" but not everything; it teaches the digging minigame and gives the player a starter ATV. — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/roblox-dig-beginners-guide)
- The player starts with the Wooden Shovel; Rocky's Shovel Shop in Fox Town sells early shovels. — [DroidGamers](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/how-to-play-roblox-dig-a-beginners-guide/)
- Dig minigame: click a patch of ground, then click while the cursor is over the ground-coloured section of a bar to fill progress; a "strong strike" on the dark centre has no cooldown and restores the heart bar; if the heart bar empties the item goes back into the ground. — [DroidGamers](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/how-to-play-roblox-dig-a-beginners-guide/)

Dig It
- The player starts with a basic shovel that only finds low-rarity items; digging is press-and-hold at a dig area, then keep the cursor inside a moving bar until a meter fills; sell by talking to a vendor and choosing "sell inventory"; default inventory is small and backpacks are bought with Doolars. — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/dig-it-a-beginner-s-guide)

99 Nights in the Forest
- Day 1 advice: chop trees near camp and feed logs to the campfire; loot nearby buildings and chests; feed junk to the grinder beside the crafting bench. If the campfire goes out the run ends. — [Blobloxpedia](https://blobloxpedia.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-beginner-guide/)
- On the first night the Deer "watches but doesn't attack"; it only hunts on later nights. — [Blobloxpedia](https://blobloxpedia.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-beginner-guide/)

Cross-game claims (unsourced opinion, use with care)

## 2. How does each game show the player what to do next without a pushy tutorial?

### Takeaway
The dominant techniques are world layout (the shop or conveyor is visible from the spawn plot), a starting wallet that fits only one purchase, one quest giver standing next to the first resource, a short always-on goal list, and other players' plots in plain view. Roblox's own guidance is a brief tutorial or contextual tooltips, not a long guided sequence.

### Cited Findings
- World layout as teacher, Grow a Garden: the player's empty plot sits "among other players' plots"; two main stores (seeds, gear) plus a cosmetic shop and pet seller are in a shared middle area. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden); [Kids Media Is Cool](https://kidsmediaiscool.substack.com/p/the-roblox-game-thats-hooked-22-million)
- Single pointer, Grow a Garden: the game "points you towards the Seed Shop" at first start, and persistent buttons at top centre teleport to the seller, so the walk is never a blocker. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- World layout as teacher, Steal a Brainrot: one large red conveyor through the centre of the map, bases around it, a lock button on the base itself. — [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/guides/166496-steal-a-brainrot-beginners-guide-tips-and-basics.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- Quest giver beside the resource, Bee Swarm Simulator: Black Bear stands near the hives and the Sunflower Field and his first quest is 100 pollen from that same field; quests step up one idea at a time (a named field, a second field, any field, red flowers, blue flowers). The colour-matching rule is only explained in quest 4 ("Red Request"). — [BSS Wiki, Black Bear](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Bear)
- Free item on accept, Bee Swarm Simulator: Mother Bear hands over 1 Treat the moment "Treat Tutorial" is accepted, so the lesson is "use this now". — [BSS Wiki, Mother Bear](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Mother_Bear)
- Free item plus one need, Adopt Me: the free Starter Egg's first need is always "Hungry" and a free sandwich is supplied, so the first task cannot fail or cost anything. — [Adopt Me Wiki](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Starter_Egg)
- Goal list, Pet Simulator 99: the account starts at Rank 1 "Noob"; each rank shows two active goals at a time (for example break N breakables, earn diamonds); finishing goals earns stars that fill a rank reward track; higher ranks add pet, egg and enchant slots. — [U7BUY guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/)
- NPC quest givers per area, Fisch: an "Angler [Quests]" NPC in each major location gives money and XP; Phineas at Moosewood gives a bait-crate quest. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fisch-roblox-beginners-guide/)
- Free vehicle, Dig: the tutorial ends by giving a starter ATV, which the guide says is enough for a long time. — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/roblox-dig-beginners-guide)
- Safe first night, 99 Nights: the main threat only watches on night 1, which lets players learn the campfire loop without dying. — [Blobloxpedia](https://blobloxpedia.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-beginner-guide/)
- Roblox official guidance: "Use a brief tutorial or contextual info like tooltips to guide users through your core loop." "Avoid long tutorials to help users get to the fun as quickly as possible." Deliver a "joyful moment" after the first completed loop, preview the progress available from repeating it, and keep the first-time experience "ideally in 5 minutes or less". — [Roblox creator docs, retention](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/analytics/retention.md)
- Roblox official method for finding pushy or broken steps: list the core loop steps, "track the completion rate of each step", and "identify and fix big drop-offs". — [Roblox creator docs, retention](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/analytics/retention.md)
- Opinion (unsourced): "Top games teach through doing"; Blox Fruits guarantees a fruit spawn in a small closed starting zone "instead of facing a tutorial wall". — [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/what-every-top-100-roblox-game-gets-right-a-design-analysis)
- Opinion (unsourced): techniques listed for tutorial-free onboarding are a constrained first environment, immediate legible feedback (within about half a second), failure as information, and one mechanic at a time; "The tutorial didn't orient them, it just delayed the moment of actual contact with your game by 45 seconds." — [RoWatcher, Onboarding Without Tutorials](https://rowatcher.com/news/onboarding-without-tutorials-teaching-through-play-in-roblox)

### Inferences
- A rank or goal list with two visible goals (PS99) is the closest proven model for "a quick easy rank-up system": it gives direction without stopping play, and each rank-up pays a slot or reward.
- None of the sources describe dim-the-screen overlays, forced menu opening, or multi-step text strips in Grow a Garden or Steal a Brainrot. The absence across several guides suggests those games do not use them, but no source states it outright.
- The Bee Swarm pattern (the rule about colours is held back until quest 4) shows a way to delay depth: rarity, mutations and tiers can each arrive as the reward text of a later goal rather than in the first minute.
- Seeing neighbours' full plots (Grow a Garden) and rich bases (Steal a Brainrot) doubles as the "preview progression" step that Roblox's docs ask for, at zero UI cost.

### Gaps
- No developer interview or RDC talk on the onboarding of these specific games was found or read in this session. Searches surfaced only analysis articles.
- The Roblox creator docs "Onboarding" design page (linked from the retention page) was not read; it likely holds more specific official advice.
- No first-hand description of glow or highlight effects on first-interaction objects in any of these games.

## 3. How fast is the first reward, first purchase, first upgrade and first new area?

### Takeaway
First purchase is immediate in Grow a Garden (20 Sheckles buys two carrot seeds) and Steal a Brainrot ($25 for $1 per second); first quest reward in Bee Swarm is 100 pollen away. First upgrades are cheap and close (a repeat-harvest strawberry; a $250 Brainrot), while the first big reset or rebirth is deliberately far away (Area 25 in PS99; a specific pair of Brainrots in Steal a Brainrot).

### Cited Findings
- Grow a Garden: first purchase is two Carrot Seeds with the 20 starting Sheckles; the first plant "doesn't take long to grow"; after "selling a few more carrots" the guide says to buy a Strawberry Seed, which can be harvested repeatedly and "won't disappear after gathering from it". — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- Grow a Garden: the seed and gear shops restock every five minutes. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/); [Kids Media Is Cool](https://kidsmediaiscool.substack.com/p/the-roblox-game-thats-hooked-22-million)
- Grow a Garden: crops (and pets) keep progressing while the player is offline. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden); [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- Steal a Brainrot price ladder for the first six Commons: Noobini Pizzanini $25 for $1/s; Lirili Larila $250 for $3/s; Tim Cheese $500 for $5/s; Fluriflura $750 for $7/s; Talpa Di Fero $1K for $9/s; Svinina Bombardino $1.25K for $10/s. Cheapest Rare: Trippi Troppi $2K for $15/s. — [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-values-belt-price-and-income-per-second-for-all-99-brainrots/)
- Steal a Brainrot: Trippi Troppi and Gangster Footera are needed for the first rebirth. — [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-values-belt-price-and-income-per-second-for-all-99-brainrots/)
- Steal a Brainrot: with three Commons, combined income reaches the next price tier in "about 8 minutes"; first rebirth is suggested at about $50M banked (AI-written summary; treat as weak). — [rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/steal-a-brainrot/beginner-guide)
- Steal a Brainrot: rebirth resets progress in exchange for better stats and extra currency; guides add permanent income multipliers, additional base slots and new gear. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot); [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/guides/166496-steal-a-brainrot-beginners-guide-tips-and-basics.html)
- Pet Simulator 99: first rebirth needs Area 25; Rebirth One gives +75% pet strength and unlocks Teleport, Auto Hatch and the Castle Key; since Update 79 rebirthing does not reset progress. — [U7BUY guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/)
- Pet Simulator 99: cheapest early upgrades are Walkspeed (2 tiers, +25%, 180 diamonds total) and Magnet (7 tiers, +32%, 48.2K diamonds total). — [U7BUY guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/)
- Bee Swarm Simulator: first quest reward is 200 honey for 100 pollen; quest rewards climb 200, 400, 500, 650, 750 honey across the first five quests. — [BSS Wiki, Black Bear](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Bear)
- Fisch: the recommended first rod upgrade is the Carbon Rod at C$2,000 from the Moosewood merchant; a rowboat costs C$700 and a GPS C$100; the first named spot beyond Moosewood is Roslit Bay. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fisch-roblox-beginners-guide/)
- Dig: charm slots unlock at level 10 (second) and level 25 (third); enchantment tomes are locked until level 30; the game suggests a $10,000 detector early, which the author calls not worth it. — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/roblox-dig-beginners-guide)
- 99 Nights: first crafting upgrades are a Map for 3 wood and Crafting Bench level 2 for 5 wood and 1 scrap; an Old Bed costs 20 wood; bench level 3 costs 15 wood and 10 scrap. — [Blobloxpedia](https://blobloxpedia.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-beginner-guide/)
- Roblox official target: the whole first-time experience, through one completed core loop and a "joyful moment", in 5 minutes or less. — [Roblox creator docs](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/analytics/retention.md)
- Opinion (unsourced): top-100 games deliver "a meaningful, visible reward within the first 60 seconds of play". — [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/what-every-top-100-roblox-game-gets-right-a-design-analysis)

### Inferences
- Steal a Brainrot arithmetic from the sourced ladder: one $25 Noobini at $1/s pays itself back in 25 seconds; the second tier ($250) is 10 times the first price, so with one or two Noobinis the player reaches the first real upgrade in roughly 2 to 4 minutes. The first purchase is instant, the second is a short, visible wait with a ticking number.
- Grow a Garden arithmetic: 20 Sheckles and a carrot seed that appears to cost 10 means the first loop is buy 2, plant 2, harvest 2, sell 2. The first repeat-harvest crop (strawberry) is the first "it now repeats by itself" upgrade and arrives after only a few carrot loops.
- Both games price progression in a single currency with a steep but smooth ladder, and gate nothing behind collection counts in the first session. That matches the direction "just coin amount" for tier unlocks.
- Rebirth or prestige is never introduced in the first three minutes in any game sourced here; it appears as a far goal (PS99 Area 25) or a named shopping list (Steal a Brainrot's two specific Brainrots).
- UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION: Grow a Garden carrots take only a few seconds to grow and sell for a little more than the seed cost each, so the first sale is inside the first minute.

### Gaps
- Carrot seed price, carrot grow time and carrot sell value: not stated in any page read (10 Sheckles is inferred from "20 Sheckles buys two"). Unread lead: https://growagarden.fandom.com/wiki/Seed_Shop .
- Pet Simulator 99 first egg price and first area price: not sourced.
- Time to first area unlock in any game, measured in minutes: not sourced.
- Steal a Brainrot first rebirth exact cash requirement and reward values: not sourced beyond the two required Brainrots.

## 4. What makes the first minute feel fun?

### Takeaway
Sources point to four things: an immediate visible gain (a per-second income number, a harvest, a hatch), a chance-based surprise early (rarity rolls on a first egg, mutations, a Rare on the belt), other players' richer plots and bases in view, and extreme simplicity. Critics describe both record-holders as simple to the point of looking unfinished, and name that simplicity as the reason they hold players.

### Cited Findings
- Screen Rant's Kaitlyn Peterson called Steal a Brainrot "addictive" because of its "lack of complexity and straightforward gameplay loop". — [Wikipedia, citing Screen Rant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- Steal a Brainrot's loop is compared by commentators to capture the flag; it spread through TikTok and YouTube stealing clips. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- Grow a Garden was called the "new generation's FarmVille" for its simplicity (Nicole Carpenter, Game File); Kotaku felt it looked more like a prototype; PCGamesN called it "a pretty standard AFK game" yet "hard to put down". The first version was built in about three days. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Grow a Garden's look and sound: studded textures that evoke older Roblox games and classical music such as Mozart's Rondo Alla Turca. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Grow a Garden "layers in new mechanics as you stick around"; "You don't start caring about them on day one, but eventually you 'level up' into these systems"; it is "simple, approachable, and lets you play at your own pace"; players can "pop in for 30 seconds to buy a rare seed and log off"; average session about 14 minutes. — [Kids Media Is Cool](https://kidsmediaiscool.substack.com/p/the-roblox-game-thats-hooked-22-million)

### Inferences
- A per-second income readout on the very first owned item (Steal a Brainrot's $1/s) makes "numbers going up" start at the moment of first purchase, before the player has learned anything else.
- The first surprise roll does not need to be in the first purchase: Grow a Garden's first carrot is deterministic, and chance (mutations, seed stock, eggs) comes later. Steal a Brainrot puts chance in the world (what appears on the belt) rather than in a menu.
- Guaranteed-common starter rolls (Adopt Me Dog or Cat) still feel like a reveal; the surprise is which one, not how rare.

## 5. How many taps and menus before the core loop repeats by itself?

### Takeaway
In the two biggest games the first loop is about four to six interactions with at most one menu, and the loop then self-sustains: Steal a Brainrot after a single purchase (income ticks with no further input), Grow a Garden after the first repeat-harvest crop and through offline growth.

### Cited Findings
- Grow a Garden first loop as described: walk to Seed Shop, buy two Carrot Seeds (one shop menu), equip seed from hotbar, click soil to plant (twice), harvest, use the top-centre teleport button to the seller, sell. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/)
- Grow a Garden self-repeat: strawberries re-harvest without replanting; plants keep growing offline. — [Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Steal a Brainrot first loop as described: go to the conveyor, buy the cheapest Brainrot, it is placed in the base and "continuously generates money". — [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/guides/166496-steal-a-brainrot-beginners-guide-tips-and-basics.html)
- Pet Simulator 99: after the first rebirth the game unlocks Auto Hatch and Teleport; before that the guide's loop is equip best pets, farm the newest area, follow rank goals. — [U7BUY guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/)
- Bee Swarm Simulator first loop: accept quest from Black Bear, collect 100 pollen in the adjacent field, return to hive to convert, return to Black Bear for 200 honey. — [BSS Wiki, Black Bear](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Bear); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/roblox-bee-swarm-simulator-how-play-bees-bears)
- Fisch first loop: cast, shake (optional), reel minigame, then sell to a merchant. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fisch-roblox-beginners-guide/)
- Dig It first loop: hold to dig, minigame, item to backpack, talk to vendor, choose sell inventory. — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/dig-it-a-beginner-s-guide)

### Inferences
- Tap count estimates (inferred from the step descriptions, not measured): Steal a Brainrot about 1 prompt press to start earning and 1 walk-over or press to collect, 0 menus. Grow a Garden about 6 to 8 taps (open shop, buy x2, close, equip, plant x2, harvest x2, sell) with 1 menu. Bee Swarm about 2 dialogue confirms plus held tool use, 1 dialogue box. Fisch and the Dig games add a skill minigame per item, so loops are longer per reward but each catch is its own small win.
- The lower the menu count before the first income, the less need for any tutorial. Steal a Brainrot's zero-menu start is the extreme case.
- "Repeats by itself" arrives through one of three devices in these games: passive per-second income (Steal a Brainrot), re-harvestable or offline-growing assets (Grow a Garden), or auto features unlocked as an early milestone reward (PS99 Auto Hatch after Rebirth One).

## 6. What do players and developers say worked or failed in onboarding?

### Takeaway
Direct developer commentary on these games' onboarding was not found in this session. What is sourced: Roblox's official docs tell developers to keep tutorials brief, reach the fun fast, and finish the first-time experience within about 5 minutes; critics credit the simplicity of Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot; and guide writers repeatedly note that the tutorials in Fisch and Dig cover basics but leave things out, which players then learn from quests, NPCs and guides.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox official: "Avoid long tutorials to help users get to the fun as quickly as possible"; use "a brief tutorial or contextual info like tooltips"; deliver a joyful moment after the first loop; preview future progress; target 5 minutes or less; measure each step's completion rate and fix big drop-offs. — [Roblox creator docs, retention](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/analytics/retention.md)
- Fisch's tutorial is "pretty good" but does not cover everything, and the reviewer recommends new players change several settings by hand. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/fisch-roblox-beginners-guide/)
- Dig's tutorial "does a decent job of covering the basics" but the game then suggests a $10,000 detector that the guide says is a poor early buy, an example of an in-game nudge players are told to ignore. — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/roblox-dig-beginners-guide)
- Grow a Garden's freedom is praised: "there's no 'right' way to play"; depth is layered in over time rather than explained up front. — [Kids Media Is Cool](https://kidsmediaiscool.substack.com/p/the-roblox-game-thats-hooked-22-million)
- Steal a Brainrot criticism is about pay-to-win items and cheating scripts, not about onboarding (Polygon, GameRant as summarised). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- Grow a Garden "doesn't typically sustain its peaks for long" (PocketGamer.biz as summarised): peaks are event-driven. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Opinion (unsourced): developers "consistently overestimate how intuitive their own games feel to strangers"; new players are seen tapping through prompts without reading in session recordings (no sample given); "The goal isn't a better tutorial. The goal is a game that doesn't need one." — [RoWatcher, Onboarding Without Tutorials](https://rowatcher.com/news/onboarding-without-tutorials-teaching-through-play-in-roblox)
- Opinion (unsourced): "Can a player pick up your game, understand it in 30 seconds, and want to do the core action again?"; Grow a Garden "launched with planting and harvesting" and added trading, prestige and events later. — [rolearn.dev](https://rolearn.dev/insights/grow-a-garden-ccu-record-analysis/)

### Inferences
- The consistent failure mode named across sources is delay before the first real action (dialogue, loading, long step lists), not lack of explanation. The consistent success pattern is one obvious affordable action, then depth revealed by later goals.
- For a catch-and-collect cafe game, the closest proven templates are: Steal a Brainrot's "first creature is nearly free and pays per second at once"; Grow a Garden's "starting wallet equals exactly the first purchase, shop in sight, neighbours visible"; Bee Swarm's "one NPC beside the first resource with a 100-unit first quest and rising rewards"; PS99's "two visible rank goals at a time, rank-ups pay slots".
- A tutorial is only justified in these games where the input itself is a skill minigame (Fisch, Dig); even there it is one guided attempt, with a free item at the end (Dig's ATV).
