# Starting wallets replace tutorials in Roblox hits

The largest Roblox collector and tycoon games of 2025 and 2026 do not run a tutorial in the first minute: they spawn the player on an owned plot with just enough money for the one cheapest item, put the shop in sight, and let the first purchase start the income. Grow a Garden hands over 20 Sheckles that buy two carrot seeds, and Steal a Brainrot's cheapest creature costs $25 and pays $1 per second from the moment it is bought. Older and deeper games add one quest giver standing beside the first resource, or one guided attempt where the input is a skill minigame, and nothing more. The evidence against forced step chains is consistent: Roblox's own example funnel lost 70% of players before the first action, a 16-step dimmed tutorial lost 41% at step one, and a 45,000-player study found tutorials did nothing or slight harm in simple games. Roblox's guidance asks for the core loop and a designed "moment of joy" inside five minutes, taught with world visuals and hints that appear only for players who stall. This matters for discovery because Home recommendations now treat a first session that ends under 60 seconds, or between 61 and 180 seconds, as a top-tier negative signal. Progression follows the same restraint: first gates are small single-currency prices, and rebirth is a distant, one-button goal that is never explained in the first three minutes. Two limits apply to everything below: no source timed any of these games with a stopwatch, and no named game has published tutorial completion or D1 figures, so timings are inferred from guides and should be confirmed by hands-on play.

## The two record-holders open with one affordable purchase and no tutorial

Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot share an opening shape that no guide describes as a tutorial. In Grow a Garden the player receives an empty plot among other players' plots and **20 Sheckles, which buy exactly two carrot seeds**; the only guidance reported is that the game "points you towards the Seed Shop", and buttons at the top of the screen teleport to the seller so the walk never blocks the loop ([Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/21/grow-a-garden-beginners-guide/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). In Steal a Brainrot the player spawns at an empty base with very little money, a large red conveyor runs through the middle of the map in view of every base, and **the cheapest creature costs $25 and earns $1 per second** ([VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/guides/166496-steal-a-brainrot-beginners-guide-tips-and-basics.html), [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-values-belt-price-and-income-per-second-for-all-99-brainrots/)). The starting wallet does the teaching: there is one sensible thing to buy, so there is no choice to get wrong. No guide read mentions a cutscene, a dimmed screen or a step list in either game, which is weak evidence of absence, not proof.

Other top games use a slightly heavier device, and the weight tracks how obvious the input is. The table summarises what the sources support.

Three patterns run through the table. First, games whose input is "walk up and press the prompt" skip the tutorial, and games with a skill minigame keep one guided attempt; a catch mechanic that is tap-to-catch belongs in the first group. Second, the restaurant games make the first income arrive without the player building anything: one working seat is enough and the customer comes to the player. Retail Tycoon 2, which needs walls, floor, roof, a counter, shelves and stock before any sale, is the outlier and is also the one with an optional tutorial ([Deltia's Gaming](https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=249723)). Third, depth is held back on purpose. Bee Swarm does not explain its colour-matching rule until the fourth quest ([BSS Wiki](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Bear)), and a Grow a Garden analysis notes that players "don't start caring" about the later systems on day one but level into them ([Kids Media Is Cool](https://kidsmediaiscool.substack.com/p/the-roblox-game-thats-hooked-22-million)).

What makes that first minute fun is simple and visible. Steal a Brainrot's per-second income starts ticking at the first purchase, before the player has learned anything else, and critics credit both hits' appeal to a lack of complexity: Screen Rant called Steal a Brainrot addictive for its "straightforward gameplay loop", and Grow a Garden, first built in about three days, was called the new generation's FarmVille ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). An analyst added that Roblox "has little patience for tutorials" and that the interface suits players "from 6 to 99" ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-grow-a-gardens-89-million-ccu)). Surprise arrives early but not necessarily in the first purchase: Bee Swarm's first egg carries visible odds of a better bee, Adopt Me's starter egg is a guaranteed common where the surprise is only dog or cat, and Grow a Garden's first carrot is fully deterministic, with chance coming later through mutations and shop stock ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/roblox-bee-swarm-simulator-how-play-bees-bears), [Adopt Me Wiki](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Starter_Egg)). Other players' fuller plots and bases sit in plain view from the spawn, which previews progression at zero interface cost.

## Step chains lose 41% to 70% at the first step; stall-only hints cost nothing

The measured losses all sit at the very first required action. Roblox staff published a funnel for an example game in which 100% of players entered the farm and only **29.52% planted a seed, a 70.48% loss at step one**, with under 3% finishing step nine; the fix that worked was an icon over the pots showing where seeds go, not more text ([Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/improving-onboarding-through-funnel-events/3064458)). A developer with a five-minute, 16-step story tutorial that dims other buttons and walks through clicks and text reported **41% quitting at step one, about 20 seconds in**, and only 30% finishing; a reply reported a click-and-read tutorial losing about half of users ([Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/41-of-players-leaving-game-within-30-seconds-of-tutorial/3814365)). The strongest controlled evidence is a CHI 2012 study of eight tutorial designs across three games and more than 45,000 players: tutorials helped only the complex, unconventional game, did nothing in one simple game, and lowered the return rate in the other (17.96% against 21.60% with no tutorial). Blocking the player gave little benefit, and a help button on demand went with 12% fewer levels completed ([Andersen et al.](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)). That study used web games and a general audience in 2011 and 2012, not children on phones, but its conclusion fits a tap-to-catch game exactly: mechanics that can be discovered by experimenting do not repay a tutorial.

The opposite failure is also documented, so "no tutorial" does not mean "no goal". A hybrid-casual publisher found that giving players up to a minute with no instruction caused confusion and churn, and that a tutorial jumping too fast between mechanics lost 60% ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/optimizing-ftue/)). The target is a clear, visible goal with no lock on the player.

Roblox's own documentation describes how to hit that target with three techniques: temporary visual cues in the world, contextual tutorials triggered by normal play, and timed hints. Its worked example is precise: if playtests show most players press the first button within 10 seconds, show the highlight at 11 seconds, once, only to players who have not acted, which helps the stuck minority "without bothering everyone else" ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques)). The same page says fixed tutorial sequences lead to boredom or frustration by restricting choice, that players skip text prompts, and that non-core features taught in onboarding are forgotten before they can be used; its Squishmallows example shows the combine tutorial only once the player holds two identical items. George Fan's ten rules from Plants vs. Zombies say the same in other words: never label a tutorial, get the player to do the action once, keep to **eight words on screen at most**, never make the player stop to dismiss a message, show tips only to players doing the wrong thing, and spread systems out, with money held back until ten levels in ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan)).

One nuance needs stating honestly. Arrows and dimming are not banned by the evidence. Roblox's docs present "spotlight the button and dim the rest" from My Hello Kitty Cafe as clearer than a text instruction, and Fan himself used a big arrow over a coin ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques)). What the loss figures condemn is chaining these into many steps that block other input and fire before the player wants the thing. A single glow on one object, at the moment the player has a reason to touch it and only if they have stalled, is consistent with every source here. No study isolates dimming, arrows or unskippable steps on their own, so this reading is a synthesis, not a measured result.

The audience sharpens all of this. Nielsen Norman Group's studies of 125 children aged 3 to 12 found that children skip instruction paragraphs, a behaviour the researchers observed on Roblox itself in 2018, that they want instant gratification and give up after a few quick tries, and that they "mine-sweep" the screen by tapping everything instead of following a main path ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/childrens-websites-usability-issues)). A 5 mm close button was too small for seven-year-olds and two left the app out of annoyance; the recommended target for young children is 2 cm square, and adult-range motor skill arrives around age 11 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/)). Roblox adds that text is too small to read comfortably on phones and that wordless teaching helps players still learning to read ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques)). The practical reading is that every button visible in minute one will be tapped, so each must do something safe and pleasant or not be shown yet, and that the tutorial must work with the text hidden.

## First gates are small coin prices and rebirth stays a far, one-button goal

Three gating models are live in top games, and the first gate in each is trivially reachable. Pet Simulator 99 prices its early areas in coins alone: **900, then 2.5k, 8k and 20k**, a rise of roughly 2.5 to 3 times per step, with no rank, rebirth or task attached to the first five ([bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/wiki/pet-simulator-99/areas)). Bee Swarm Simulator gates fields on a count of bees owned (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 35) and makes the first gate forgiving: it alone does not teleport an under-qualified player back to spawn ([bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/wiki/bee-swarm-simulator/fields), [BSS Wiki](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Bee_Gate)). Steal a Brainrot demands named creatures, but only at rebirth: the first needs $500K plus two specific Brainrots, and from the sixth rebirth the game drops to one named creature each ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/steal-a-brainrot-rebirth-guide-levels-and-rewards/)). Its ordinary purchase ladder is pure cash, from $25 through $250, $500, $750 and $1K to a $2K first Rare ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/steal-a-brainrot-values-belt-price-and-income-per-second-for-all-99-brainrots/)). Lumber Tycoon 2, Work at a Pizza Place and Restaurant Tycoon 3 are likewise price tags all the way up ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/roblox-lumber-tycoon-2-how-to-play-from-first-axe-to-first-sawmill/), [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/restaurant-tycoon-3-a-beginner-s-guide)).

No public data compares retention between currency gates and collection gates; that question is unanswered. The design reasoning still favours coins for the opening. A collection gate can leave a player holding the money and still blocked by luck or by not knowing where a named item comes from, while a price is a single number that is always moving. Pet Simulator 99 shows how to keep a collection system without making it a wall: movement through the world is paid in coins, while its rank system shows **two goals at a time** and pays out slots and rewards instead of blocking the road ([U7BUY](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/), [bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/wiki/pet-simulator-99/ranks)). Roblox's guidance points the same way, asking for low early thresholds so players level up quickly and for starting currency tuned by experiment ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding)).

Rank up and rebirth are made quick by collapsing the requirement and the action into one control. Steal a Brainrot keeps a Rebirth button on the left of the screen from the start; pressing it shows what is needed, and pressing it again when ready performs the rebirth ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/steal-a-brainrot-rebirth-guide-levels-and-rewards/)). Pet Simulator 99 puts a shrine in the player's path with a single green button ([holdtoreset.com](https://holdtoreset.com/pet-simulator-99-rebirth-system-explained/)). In both, the rewards listed first are capabilities, not multipliers: an extra base slot and new gear in one, auto hatch and a teleport in the other. None of the games sourced introduces rebirth in the first three minutes; it sits far off, at Area 25 in Pet Simulator 99. Sources conflict on whether Pet Simulator 99's rebirth still resets progress, with the more recent accounts saying it does not ([U7BUY](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-beginner-guide/), [bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/wiki/pet-simulator-99/rebirths)). No source describes the celebration effects at any game's rank up, so that part needs direct play or video review.

The reason to return tomorrow is planted by the first action, not by a popup. Grow a Garden prints "Grows Offline" on its icon, crops keep growing while the player is away, and its operator credits weekly Saturday updates with a gift for bringing more players back each week ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-grow-a-gardens-89-million-ccu)). Restaurant Tycoon 2 takes the older, opposite line, leaving cash on tables that despawns unless collected every 30 to 45 seconds ([narcosis-the-game.com](https://www.narcosis-the-game.com/?p=2002)). For a young audience under a no-dark-patterns rule, the first direction fits and the second does not.

## Home ranks first sessions on 60-second and 180-second bounce bands

Since June 2026, Roblox's "Recommended for You" sort ranks on eight per-user signals over a 28-day view. The four it calls most important are play through rate, **first play bounce rate (a negative signal with published bands at under 60 seconds and 61 to 180 seconds)**, play days per user, and playtime per user capped at 60 minutes per day ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery.md)). Play through rate and bounce are measured only on a player's first session arriving organically from Home, and new games do not wait 28 days: these two signals "kick in immediately" ([Discovery FAQ](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery-faq.md)). Roblox replaced the older 7-day view and split up the former qualified play through rate signal so that games which retain players are favoured over those that "win attention with exciting thumbnails" ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox)). Spend signals sit in the second tier, and Roblox's stated order is to fix retention first. Any developer guide that names QPTR as the signal or cites a 7-day window is out of date, and Roblox has never published a numeric definition of a "qualified play".

This gives the first three minutes a hard, measurable job: a Home-sourced player who leaves inside 60 seconds lands in the worst band, and one who leaves inside three minutes still counts as a bounce. Anything that blocks between join and first input spends that budget. Roblox states that long join times hurt acquisition and retention and that players leave before entering ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization)). One developer with about 5% D1 found nearly half of joiners never reached the first tutorial step, with a blocking preload screen timed at over a minute blamed in replies ([Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/stuck-on-low-retention-players-leave-before-playing-the-tutorial/4557364)). Roblox does not document whether the bounce timer starts at clicking Play, at join, or at spawn, so how much loading time counts is unknown; the safe assumption is that all of it does.

Roblox's onboarding guidance lines up with these signals. The first-time experience should deliver the core loop "ideally in 5 minutes or less", end on a designed reward or celebration, and show short, mid and long-term goals so the player leaves wanting more ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/retention), [Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding.md)). Each step should be logged as a one-time onboarding funnel event from the server, and the largest, earliest drop fixed first, ahead of new content ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/funnel-events), [Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/improving-onboarding-through-funnel-events/3064458)).

Benchmarks deserve caution. Roblox publishes no absolute retention targets; it shows each game a private comparison with similar experiences or its genre, and since October 2025 lets creators split D1, D7 and D30 by acquisition source ([Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/analytics-view-retention-by-acquisition-source-and-select-your-benchmark-set/4010157)). The only public dataset found, covering games that use the GameAnalytics SDK, puts **median D1 between 4.3% and 11.5%** depending on average session length, with a median session of 6.62 minutes and a quarter of sessions ending before 2.36 minutes ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-roblox-report)). Blog figures of 20% to 30% D1 for tycoons cite no dataset and disagree with it by a factor of two to three. The right yardstick is the game's own percentile in Creator Analytics, filtered to the Home Recommendations source. No named Roblox game has published a before-and-after retention result from an onboarding change, so the team's own funnel and an Experiments A/B test are the only reliable evidence available.

## A cat cafe's first three minutes: catch, earn, rank up, with help only on a stall

The following is a design synthesis from the evidence above, not a published benchmark, and every timing is a starting value to test. The first minute should be the fun verb itself. The player spawns with control at once, inside or at the door of their own small cafe, with two or three easy cats in view a few steps away and no cutscene, modal or blocking load, mirroring Pokemon GO's opening of three catchable creatures and almost no instruction ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/is-pok-mon-go-s-ux-intentionally-obtuse-)). The first catch cannot fail: no escape chance and a short catch time, since Roblox's fishing example names high escape odds and a too-advanced rare target as onboarding leaks ([Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/improving-onboarding-through-funnel-events/3064458)). The caught cat then does what Steal a Brainrot's first creature does: it goes to the cafe and begins earning a visible per-second amount, and a first customer arrives on their own as in the restaurant tycoons. The same fishing example shows that the sell step is where catching games lose people, so the first conversion to coins should happen automatically or in one tap where the player already stands. Each action gets layered feedback, a flash, a number and coins flying, because players who see no confirmation assume the game is broken ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques)).

| Window | What the player does | What the game shows |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 10 s | Has control; sees cats and their own cafe | Nothing on screen but the world and a coin counter |
| By about 30 s | Catches a first cat that cannot escape | Reveal and celebration; the cat walks to the cafe and starts earning |
| By about 60 s | Sees coins tick; a customer arrives unprompted | One goal chip of eight words or fewer that ticks with progress |
| 1 to 3 min | Catches two or three more; affords the first rank up | One rank-up button showing one coin price, lit when affordable |
| About 3 min | Presses rank up once | A set-piece celebration, one new capability, and the next area in view with its price |

Guidance follows a help ladder in which each rung fires once and only on a stall. The world comes first: the nearest cat is the brightest moving thing in view. Second is one small goal chip that never blocks input and counts progress, the same device as Pet Simulator 99's two visible goals. Third, about a second after the time by which most playtesters have acted, a glow or ground trail appears on the target, as in Roblox's 10 and 11 second example. Only after a longer stall does a guide character speak, and then in a motivating voice instead of a bare instruction, since "help me rebuild my house" outperforms "tap the house" ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/optimizing-ftue/)). There is no numbered step strip, no dimmed screen sequence and no "?" button as the safety net.

Everything else waits for its trigger. Shop, codes, daily rewards, rebirth and any Robux offer stay hidden until play calls for them, both because children tap every visible button and because spend is a second-tier Home signal that is not worth a first-tier bounce. The first rank up costs roughly what the first two or three catches earn, is priced in coins alone, resets nothing, and pays a capability such as a cat slot or a second seat ahead of a multiplier. Rarity is taught by feeling it: one early catch is noticeably better than expected. Near-miss catch mechanics are gambling-adjacent and no source addresses their fit for a young audience, so they need a policy check before any use. The session closes its opening by pointing at tomorrow in the Grow a Garden way, with something in the cafe that visibly keeps improving while the player is away.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the task. The current tutorial does not need better steps; it needs fewer reasons to exist. Top games solved onboarding with economy and level design, a wallet that fits one purchase, a shop in sight and a first creature that pays immediately, and kept guidance for the minority who stall. For a cat catching cafe, the catch is already an input a child understands without words, which places the game in the class where tutorials measurably do not help. The owner's direction of coin-only tier unlocks and a quick rank up matches what the largest games do in their first session, with the caveat that no public data proves coin gates retain better than collection gates.

Three things remain unknown and are cheap to settle. A timed viewing of one first-play video each for Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot, Pet Simulator 99 and My Hello Kitty Cafe would replace the inferred timings with real ones, and would show how the catch games that were not covered here (Pet Catchers, Catch and Tame, Creatures of Sonaria) open. A server-side funnel of six to nine first-session events, starting with "joined" to "first input" as its own step, would show where this game actually loses players before any step is rewritten. And because Roblox does not say when the bounce timer starts, the join-to-control time on a low-end phone should be measured and treated as part of the 60 seconds.
